How the peg pyramid works

The board is a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row one peg wider than the row above it. A ball dropped on top has an even chance of moving left or right at each peg, and it repeats that choice on the way down. After the final row, it lands in one of the bottom pockets - always one more pocket than there are rows.

Because every route is made up of fifty-fifty turns, most balls gather around the middle and only a few reach the far edges. Mathematicians call this a Galton board, and the pattern it creates is a binomial distribution. In simple terms, the payout table flips that curve: central pockets are common but small, while edge pockets are rare but larger.
